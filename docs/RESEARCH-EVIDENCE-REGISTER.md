# Sabi research evidence register

**Research cut-off:** 26 November 2026  
**Purpose:** source-linked evidence queue for Sabi editors. This is not a scorecard and does not authorise automatic publication.

## 1. State fiscal and service-delivery evidence

### 2025 fiscal performance

BudgIT's *2026 State of States* report assesses the 2025 fiscal year across 35 states. Rivers State is excluded because the report says the state did not produce a 2025 audited financial statement or budget implementation reports. BudgIT says it used audited reports for revenue and expenditure, Q4 budget implementation reports for critical-sector spending, and Debt Management Office data at 31 December 2025 for debt comparisons.

- Report and methodology: https://budgit.org/post_publications/from-allocation-to-impact-rethinking-state-spending-priorities-2026-state-of-states-report/
- Full report: https://budgit.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/States26SMDF1.pdf
- Example fiscal observations: Lagos internally generated revenue was reported at ₦1.71tn; Enugu IGR increased from ₦180.5bn in 2024 to ₦407.76bn in 2025 (+125.91%).

**Interpretation rule:** fiscal capacity, IGR, debt and budget execution are not governor performance scores. They are inputs to fiscal analysis. They do not independently establish service quality, project completion or improved household outcomes. Rivers' missing reports are a data-availability and transparency gap, not proof of a positive or negative outcome.

### Health outcome signal: childhood vaccination

The 2024 State of Health of the Nation report, using the 2023–24 NDHS, reports full basic vaccination among children aged 12–23 months at 39% nationally, compared with 31% in 2018. Bayelsa increased from 18.0% to 63.4% (+45.4 percentage points). Gombe, Bauchi, Jigawa, Ogun and Katsina more than doubled their previous survey values. Sokoto increased from 4.6% to 8.4%, still below the national estimate. Eight states showed declines.

Source: https://fmohconnect.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/The-State-of-Health-of-the-Nation-Report-2024.pdf.

**Interpretation rule:** NDHS survey estimates and DHIS2 administrative coverage are different data series and must not be combined. Some mortality indicators use multi-year look-back periods and should not be attributed to a governor's short tenure.

## 2. Federal executive: 2023 manifesto and delivery evidence

### Original manifesto and independent tracker

The 2023 APC manifesto is *Renewed Hope 2023: Action Plan for a Better Nigeria*, published 21 October 2022. OpenPolls' independent tracker identifies 88 commitments, of which 22 have numerical targets or deadlines. Its tracker statuses include laws, budget lines and launched/underway items; these are evidence-status categories, not completed-promise scores.

- Original manifesto tracker: https://openpolls.africa/manifestos/2023/apc
- Manifesto archive: https://openpolls.africa/manifestos?year=2023
- Independent compilation of campaign promises: https://www.ipcng.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/IPC.-DOCUMENTED-CAMPAIGN-PROMISES-OF-PRESIDENT-TINUBU.pdf

**Interpretation rule:** OpenPolls is a discovery aid, not Sabi's scoring authority. Reconstruct the original promise set, catalogue it, then verify every status against primary records. Do not grade the 2023–2027 term against a separate 2027–2031 campaign manifesto.

### Health-sector claims

On 13 November 2026, the Federal Ministry of Health said all 41 presidential health-sector commitments had been achieved. The release cites cumulative BHCPF releases, PHC revitalisation, supported facilities, insurance coverage and skilled-birth-attendant recruitment, while also acknowledging continuing problems in stunting, affordability, care quality and patient satisfaction.

Source: https://health.gov.ng/tinubu-administration-achieves-all-41-presidential-health-sector-commitments-pate/

**Status:** official ministry self-report; not an independently verified Sabi finding. Obtain the complete list of 41 commitments, the original baselines and deadlines, release-level financial records, facility-level verification and outcome data before assigning any commitment status.

### Electricity generation

NERC figures previously reviewed indicate average grid generation of about 3,929 MW in 2022 and 4,144 MW in 2024; October 2026 average hourly grid generation was reported at about 4,047 MW.

Sources: https://nerc.gov.ng/ and the relevant NERC quarterly reports.

**Interpretation rule:** these grid figures do not substantiate a claim of doubled grid generation on this measure. Before assessing the manifesto pledge, verify its exact wording and scope, including whether it refers to installed capacity, available capacity, grid generation, distributed generation or off-grid supply. Do not conflate these measures.

## 4. Scoring readiness and evidence gaps

1. Do not publish a governor's Integrity Score until at least 60% of the identifiable commitments in that governor's original manifesto/pledge set have been catalogued. Show catalogue coverage separately from evidence coverage.
2. Do not publish an Impact Score until at least 3 of 5 dimensions and 60% of planned indicator weight are assessable.
3. Do not translate fiscal rankings, budget execution or ministry press releases into overall performance scores.
4. For every state, collect the original manifesto, budget and Q4 implementation report, audited accounts, procurement and project records, and comparable outcome series.
5. Rivers requires special source work because the cited BudgIT report excludes it for missing audited and implementation reports. Seek state records, Auditor-General reports, legislative records, procurement evidence and credible independent reporting; label unresolved gaps.
6. No politician's Integrity or Impact score has been calculated or changed by this evidence register.

## 5. Source-quality hierarchy

1. Original legal, INEC, court, budget, audited-account, regulator and official statistical records.
2. State and federal ministry / agency data, with underlying tables and dates.
3. Independent fiscal and sector research with published methods and downloadable datasets.
4. Credible reporting for context and cross-checking.
5. Campaign material and press releases as evidence of what was promised or claimed, not proof that delivery occurred.
